## The short answer

If your goal is to get as close as practical to Rogan's documented high-heat routine, start with a **traditional sauna** that can reliably operate in the 175–190°F range. If installation or budget makes that unrealistic, infrared and portable units are easier compromises.

## Best match: a traditional home sauna

A traditional sauna is the closest match because it heats the **room air** to the high temperatures Rogan commonly discusses. For a home setup, pay more attention to the fundamentals than to flashy wellness claims.

Look for adequate heater power for the cabin volume, proper ventilation, sauna-rated construction, a dependable control system, heater stones where appropriate, a useful warranty and enough bench height to sit in the hotter portion of the room.

## Infrared: easier, but a different experience

Infrared saunas are popular because many models are designed for straightforward indoor installation and run at lower air temperatures. That can be a feature, not a flaw, if convenience means you will use the sauna more consistently.

But do not buy an infrared unit assuming it will feel identical to a traditional sauna at 180°F or more. It will not.

## Portable sauna: the budget test

If you have never owned a sauna, spending thousands before knowing whether you will use it is a big leap. Portable steam or infrared-style enclosures can be a cheaper way to test whether deliberate heat exposure fits your routine.

They are not a true substitute for a well-built traditional sauna, but they solve a different problem: **low-cost access to heat at home**.

## Sauna blanket: smallest footprint

A sauna blanket does not recreate the social or whole-room experience of a sauna. Its appeal is footprint and storage. For apartments, smaller homes or people who simply want a compact heat product, that may matter more than copying Rogan's exact setup.

## What we would prioritize before buying

1. **Where it will go.** Measure the space, doorway and ceiling height.
2. **Electrical requirements.** Larger traditional heaters may need dedicated electrical work.
3. **Actual temperature capability.** Do not assume every product marketed as a sauna reaches traditional-sauna temperatures.
4. **Ventilation and construction.** A sauna is a heat appliance, not just furniture.
5. **Warranty and service.** This matters more as purchase price rises.
6. **Frequency of use.** The best sauna is not the most expensive one; it is the safe, appropriate unit you will consistently use.

## What temperature should you target?

Rogan has discussed roughly **185°F for about 20 minutes** as a personal sweet spot after experimenting with hotter temperatures. That does not mean a beginner should immediately copy it.

## Bottom line

**Traditional sauna** is the best match to the high-temperature Rogan routine. **Infrared** wins on easier home installation for many buyers. **Portable units and blankets** are lower-cost ways to find out whether you will actually make heat exposure a habit.

High heat is not a competition. Sauna can cause dehydration, dizziness and blood-pressure changes. Avoid combining heavy alcohol use with sauna, leave if you feel unwell, and get individualized medical guidance when a health condition or medication affects heat tolerance.
