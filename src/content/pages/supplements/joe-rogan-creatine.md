## Creatine powder vs. gummies

The choice is mostly about **cost, convenience and serving size**.

### Powder

Powder is the straightforward option. You scoop it into water, coffee, a shake or another drink. Plain monohydrate products also make it easy to compare labels without paying for flavoring, candy-style formats or elaborate blends.

**Best for:** value, simple ingredient lists and people who already make a daily shake or drink.

[Shop plain creatine monohydrate](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=100%25+creatine+monohydrate+powder)

### Gummies

Gummies can be easier to travel with and more pleasant if you dislike mixing powder. The tradeoff is that you may need several gummies per serving, and the cost per gram of creatine can be higher.

**Best for:** convenience and people who are more likely to consistently use a chewable product.

## What type should you buy?

For most people shopping this category, **creatine monohydrate** is the benchmark. It has a much deeper research history than the more exotic forms marketed as upgrades.

A sensible label checklist is short:

- Creatine monohydrate is clearly listed.
- The amount per serving is easy to understand.
- There is no giant proprietary blend hiding the dose.
- The product comes from a seller and manufacturer you trust.
- The cost per serving makes sense for something you may use consistently.

## Does creatine actually work?

Creatine is one of the better-established sports supplements for improving performance during repeated high-intensity exercise and supporting gains from resistance training. Research on cognition is also interesting, but the strength and exercise-performance case is more established than treating creatine as a universal brain booster.
