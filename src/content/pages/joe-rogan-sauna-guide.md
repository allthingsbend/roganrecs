Joe Rogan's sauna routine is simple enough to explain in one sentence: **roughly 185°F for about 20 minutes** is the clearest recent protocol he has described publicly.

The internet managed to turn that into a complicated religion.

> **The short version:** if you want the closest thing to Rogan's setup, buy or use a **traditional high-heat sauna**, not a steam room. You do not need to start at 185°F, you do not need to hit 200°F, and you definitely do not need to turn heat exposure into a toughness contest.

## Why 185°F and 20 minutes became the Rogan number

In **JRE #2170 with Max Lugavere**, Rogan described experimenting with temperatures as high as about 205°F. He also described feeling wiped out and irritating his throat after pushing the heat too far.

His conclusion was more useful than the macho version people repeat online: **185°F for about 20 minutes felt like enough stress without being stupid about it**.

[Read the JRE #2170 transcript](https://jre.ai/episodes/2170-max-lugavere/transcript)

Older JRE discussions with Dr. Rhonda Patrick included numbers around **180°F for 20 to 25 minutes**, so the general pattern is consistent even though the exact routine has changed.

## Traditional vs infrared vs steam: do not lump them together

| Type | Typical feel | Best for | Rogan match |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Traditional sauna** | Hot air, usually lower humidity | High-heat sauna experience | **Closest match** |
| **Infrared sauna** | Lower room temperature, radiant heat | Easier indoor installation | Partial match |
| **Portable sauna** | Compact, lower-cost heat exposure | Testing the habit cheaply | Budget option |
| **Steam room** | Very high humidity | People who enjoy steam | Different modality |

Traditional sauna is the obvious choice if your goal is specifically to recreate the high-heat routine Rogan talks about.

Infrared is not fake sauna. It is just different. If a plug-in infrared cabin fits your house and you actually use it four times a week, that may be a better purchase than a traditional sauna you never install correctly.

## What the science actually supports

Sauna has a more interesting evidence base than a lot of wellness gadgets, but the strongest claims still need context.

### Cardiovascular outcomes

A well-known Finnish cohort study followed 2,315 middle-aged men for more than 20 years and found that more frequent sauna bathing was associated with lower rates of sudden cardiac death, cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality.

[Read the study on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25705824/)

That is meaningful, but it is **observational evidence**. It does not prove that buying a sauna causes the entire reduction.

### Heat stress is real

Sauna raises skin temperature, heart rate and blood flow. Your body is dealing with a real thermoregulatory load.

That does not make sauna exercise. It does not train muscle, bone or aerobic capacity in the same way actual training does.

### Heat shock proteins

Heat shock proteins are real, and heat can activate heat-stress pathways. What gets sloppy is when that mechanism gets stretched into claims that every sauna session is reversing aging or detoxifying your cells.

Interesting mechanism, not magic spell.

### Recovery

Many people, Rogan included, report that sauna feels good after training. Heat can feel relaxing and may reduce the perception of stiffness.

That is a perfectly good reason to use it. You do not need to invent a dramatic muscle-regeneration claim to justify something that makes you feel better and helps you recover mentally.

## Does sauna detox you?

Not in the way supplement ads use the word.

You sweat. Sweat contains water, electrolytes and trace amounts of other substances. Your liver and kidneys still do the heavy lifting for normal detoxification.

Use sauna because you enjoy heat exposure, relaxation, the ritual or the cardiovascular stress. Those are more defensible reasons than saying you are sweating out every bad decision you made this weekend.

## Sauna plus cold plunge

Rogan often pairs sauna with cold exposure. That does not mean cold is required for sauna to work.

A simple contrast routine might be:

1. sauna
2. cool down or cold plunge
3. rest
4. repeat only if you feel good and have experience with it

For the cold side, see our [Joe Rogan cold plunge guide](/joe-rogan-cold-plunge-guide).

If your main goal is hypertrophy, remember that frequent immediate post-lifting cold-water immersion may interfere with some training adaptations. Sauna does not create the same concern.

## How hot should a beginner go?

Do not start with Rogan's number because Rogan said Rogan's number.

A better progression is:

- start with a shorter session at a manageable temperature
- learn how you respond
- increase **time or temperature**, not everything at once
- leave before dizziness, nausea or confusion
- rehydrate afterward

The goal is repeatable heat exposure, not winning a contest nobody is scoring.

## Is a home sauna worth it?

A home sauna is worth considering when **convenience changes your behavior**.

If the nearest sauna is 25 minutes away and a home unit means you use it four times a week, the economics become more reasonable.

If you already belong to a gym with a good sauna five minutes away, buying one for your house may be completely unnecessary.

### Buy one if

- you already use sauna consistently
- you have appropriate space and power
- you value privacy and convenience
- multiple people in the household will use it
- the cost does not force you to compromise elsewhere

### Skip it if

- you are buying because sauna suddenly became your new personality
- you have never used one consistently
- installation is a mess
- you already have easy gym access
- you are stretching your budget for theoretical health optimization

## Safety

High heat can cause dehydration, dizziness and blood-pressure changes. Avoid sauna when heavily intoxicated, and do not combine extreme heat with dehydration just because somebody online called it hormesis.

Leave immediately if you feel faint, confused, nauseated, weak or otherwise unwell. People with cardiovascular disease, unstable medical conditions, pregnancy-related concerns or medications that affect blood pressure or fluid balance should get individualized medical guidance.

[Read a sauna safety review on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31102597/)
