If there's one food Rogan would eat for the rest of his life, he's said it's elk. And the thing he cooks it on is a Traeger.

That's not a sponsor read. In 2019 Rogan posted a photo of a Sunday elk roast "fresh off the Traeger," eaten with avocado and red wine, with a thank-you to Cameron Hanes and John Dudley for turning him into a hunter. The method behind it has been written up in detail since, and it's simple enough that you can copy it on a Tuesday night with grocery store beef.

> **The short version:** Rogan uses a **Traeger pellet grill** (JRE Library reports a [Traeger Ironwood 650](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B9VBFRLG)). His method is a **reverse sear**: smoke at 275°F until the center hits 120 to 125°F, then sear hard for about 45 seconds a side. A **wireless thermometer** does the real work. The grill is the least important part.

## Which Traeger does Rogan have?

The cleanest evidence is his own post: the elk came off a Traeger. The specific model comes from JRE Library, which tracks his gear and lists a **Traeger Ironwood 650** as current. Before that, Rogan cooked on a Traeger Timberline, a Yoder YS640 and Green Mountain grills.

Two honest caveats. First, the model number is reported secondhand, not something Rogan has read off the lid on camera. Second, Traeger has redesigned the Ironwood line since the 650 came out, so "buy the exact Rogan grill" mostly means "buy the current Ironwood."

### Which Traeger should you actually buy?

- **Want the closest match:** the current [Traeger Ironwood](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B9VBFRLG). It's the successor to the 650, with Wi-Fi control and Traeger's Super Smoke mode.
- **Feeding a crowd:** the [Ironwood XL](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B9ZZ2BGW) gives you a lot more grate space.
- **Want the old 650 specifically:** some sellers still bundle remaining [Ironwood 650 stock with pellets](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DK2L6KNB). Check that it's new, not refurbished.

If you're not sure you'll smoke often, skip the Traeger and try the method in your oven first. A pellet grill is a convenience upgrade. It's not what makes the meat good.

## The method: reverse sear, step by step

This comes from a write-up of how Rogan cooks, pieced together from his Instagram posts and a conversation on John Dudley's Nock On podcast (episode 100) with Chad Ward. It works on any cut at least an inch thick: steaks, tenderloin, backstrap, roasts.

1. **Season early.** Salt, pepper, garlic powder. If you have time, salt the meat an hour or more ahead.
2. **Smoke low at 275°F.** Put a probe in the thickest part.
3. **Pull at 120 to 125°F internal.** This is why the thermometer matters. Wild game is lean, and the window between perfect and dry is a few degrees.
4. **Sear hard.** Cast iron or a grill as hot as it gets, with a little butter or oil. About **45 seconds per side**.
5. **Serve around 135°F.** That's medium-rare. Let it rest a few minutes first.

Why it works: cooking low first brings the whole piece up evenly, so you don't get the gray band you'd get from searing first. The sear at the end is only there for crust.

### Why elk needs this more than beef

Elk has very little fat marbled through it. Beef forgives you for overshooting by ten degrees. Elk doesn't; it goes from rosy to liver-like fast. The reverse sear plus a probe thermometer is how you avoid wasting meat someone spent a week in the mountains for.

No elk? The method is identical with a thick ribeye, tri-tip or venison from a local processor.

## The thermometer is the real purchase

If you buy one thing from this page, make it a wireless probe thermometer. Rogan's is an [Ivation long-range wireless model](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00ANCXJR6): a probe stays in the meat while you watch the temperature from inside the house.

Any good dual-probe wireless thermometer does the same job. What matters is that it reads the inside of the meat, so you're pulling at 120 to 125°F on the number, not on a guess.

[Check the Ivation wireless thermometer on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00ANCXJR6)

## About the seasoning

Rogan's method uses plain kosher salt, black pepper and garlic powder, and he's also used **Flavor God Garlic Lovers**, a garlic-heavy blend with Himalayan salt.

It's a fine shortcut if you like garlic. It's also exactly the kind of thing that doesn't matter much. Good salt, enough of it, applied early, beats any blend.

[Check Flavor God Garlic Lovers on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CG7N8VD2)

## What we'd skip

- **Buying a pellet grill to "cook like Rogan" before trying the oven.** Same method, same result, no new appliance.
- **Proprietary "elk rubs."** Salt, pepper, garlic. That's what he uses.
- **Cooking to a time instead of a temperature.** Every piece of wild game is a different thickness. The probe is the only thing that tells you when.
