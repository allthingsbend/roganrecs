Rogan's studio gym is mostly what you'd expect: Rogue rigs, kettlebells, a heavy bag wall, an Iron Neck. Tucked into the inventory is one small, cheap, very humbling thing: an **IronMind Captains of Crush No. 1** hand gripper.

It's the most affordable piece of gear in the JRE gym, and the most misunderstood. People buy the "Rogan one," fail to close it, and assume the gripper is broken. It isn't. It's rated at around 140 lb.

> **The short version:** Rogan's studio gym lists a [Captains of Crush No. 1](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00938VCCA) (about 140 lb). Unless you already train grip, start with the [Trainer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002U3CNU) (about 100 lb) and make the No. 1 your goal. Two or three short sessions a week is enough.

## What's in Rogan's gym, and where that comes from

JRE Library keeps a detailed inventory of Rogan's studio gym, down to plate brands and bag models. The grip section has exactly one item: the **Captains of Crush No. 1**, listed at 140 lb resistance.

That fits. Rogan is a jiu-jitsu black belt and a lifelong martial artist, and crushing grip shows up everywhere in grappling. And a gripper takes up no space at all.

## The Captains of Crush ladder

IronMind rates each gripper by roughly how much force it takes to close. The numbers go up fast:

| Gripper | Approx. rating | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Guide | 60 lb | Never trained grip, smaller hands, rehab |
| Sport | 80 lb | Beginners who want an easy start |
| **Trainer** | **100 lb** | **Where most adults who lift should start** |
| **No. 1** | **140 lb** | **Rogan's. A real goal for most people** |
| No. 1.5 | 167.5 lb | Committed grip trainees |
| No. 2 | 195 lb | Very strong hands |
| No. 3 and up | 280 lb and beyond | Elite. IronMind certifies the few who close these under strict rules |

The jump from Trainer to No. 1 is 40 lb, which is why going straight to the No. 1 stalls so many people.

## Which one to buy

- **You lift, but never trained grip:** start with the [Trainer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002U3CNU). You'll probably close it, and the work is in doing it cleanly for reps.
- **You've never trained, or have smaller hands:** the [Guide](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002U46IK) is a sensible, cheap starting point.
- **You can already close a Trainer for sets:** you're ready for the [No. 1](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00938VCCA).
- **Want both directions:** IronMind sells a [No. 1 set with Expand-Your-Hand bands](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09ZGR6GWG), which train the opening muscles grippers ignore. Good for elbow health.

## How to train with it without hurting your elbows

Grippers look harmless. They're not. They put a lot of load through small tendons in the forearm, and the most common way people get hurt is by squeezing to failure every day.

1. **Two or three sessions a week.** Not daily.
2. **Short and focused.** A few sets of 5 to 10 reps on a gripper you can close, plus a few attempts on one you can't.
3. **Set it deep.** Start with the handles as close together as you can set them in your hand. That's how the ratings are meant to be tested.
4. **Train the other direction too.** A rubber band around your fingers, opened against resistance, balances things out.
5. **Stop at sharp pain.** Dull forearm fatigue is normal. Pain at the elbow is your cue to back off for a week.

### Grippers and jiu-jitsu

A gripper builds crushing strength, the short, hard squeeze. In jiu-jitsu, a lot of grip work is endurance: holding sleeves, collars and wrists for minutes. Grippers help, but pair them with dead hangs, towel pull-ups or rope climbs if grappling is the goal.
