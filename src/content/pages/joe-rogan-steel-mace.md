## Why a mace feels heavier than it weighs

All the weight of a mace sits at the end of a long handle. The farther your hands are from the ball, the more leverage it has on you. A 10 lb mace swung from the end of the handle feels nothing like a 10 lb dumbbell.

That's the point. It trains your grip, shoulders and core to control a load that's constantly trying to pull you off balance. It's also why starting too heavy is the most common mistake.

## Which weight to buy

- **New to swinging tools, or smaller:** [7 lb](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0792G8TDM)
- **Most adults who lift:** [10 lb](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B012XC0CY6). This is the best first mace for most people
- **Comfortable with kettlebells and want room to grow:** [15 lb](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B012XC0LDI)
- **Experienced, chasing Rogan's top end:** [25 lb](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B012XCYXL4) or the [Quad Mace](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B012XCQ786)

If you're unsure between two, pick the lighter one. You'll grip it closer to the head while learning, then slide your hands toward the end as you get stronger, which makes the same mace harder.

## Three moves to learn first

1. **Mace squat with front hold.** Hold the mace upright in front of you, ball on top, and squat. It teaches you to keep the mace stacked and controlled.
2. **10-to-2 swing.** Swing the mace behind your head and back to the front, stopping at "10 o'clock" and "2 o'clock" on each side, like a clock face. This is the foundation for everything else.
3. **The 360.** The full circle around your head and back. This is the signature mace move. Learn it light, with your hands close to the head of the mace, and only extend once it's smooth.

### Before you swing

- **Check overhead clearance.** You need ceiling height and a clear space around you. Low basement ceilings and maces don't mix.
- **Grip it, don't strangle it.** Over-gripping wrecks your forearms fast.
- **Go slow first.** Control the path before adding speed. A mace that gets away from you hits the floor, the wall, or you.

## Maces vs. clubs

Clubs are shorter, shaped like a bowling pin, and often swung one at a time. They're excellent for **shoulder mobility** and are a gentler starting point if your shoulders are cranky. Maces are better for **rotational power** and two-handed strength.

If you only buy one, buy a mace. If your shoulders need work, start with a light pair of clubs.
