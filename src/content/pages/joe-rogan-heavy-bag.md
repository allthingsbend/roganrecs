In January 2022, Rogan posted a short clip of himself throwing spinning kicks into a heavy bag. He was 54. The video went everywhere, and the replies were full of professional fighters, including Fabricio Werdum, Edson Barboza and Liam Harrison, reacting to how hard he was hitting.

It shouldn't have been a surprise. Before comedy and commentary, Rogan was a serious taekwondo competitor, and kicking a bag is still part of how he trains. His studio gym has a whole wall of them.

> **The short version:** Rogan's gym has a [Fairtex banana bag](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D4W3X935) plus several Fuji bags on a trolley rack. If you want one bag for kicking and punching at home, a **six-foot banana bag** like the Fairtex HB6 is the pick. Spend as much thought on how you'll hang it as on the bag.

## Why kickers use long bags

A standard boxing bag stops around waist height. That's fine for punching. It's useless for low kicks.

A **banana bag** runs about six feet, so it hangs low enough to kick at thigh and calf height, high enough for head-height kicks, and has room for knees and punches in between. That's why it's the default bag in Muay Thai gyms. If you only buy one bag, and you kick at all, this is the shape.

## Which bag to buy

- **You kick and punch, and want one bag:** the [Fairtex HB6 banana bag](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D4W3X935). It's the same brand as the banana bag in Rogan's gym and a staple in Muay Thai gyms.
- **You want to save on shipping and don't mind work:** the [unfilled HB6](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B006CV65R8). Plan on a lot of fabric to fill it evenly, packed tight so it doesn't settle into a hard bottom.
- **You only punch:** a regular boxing heavy bag is cheaper and does the job. Don't pay for length you won't use.

## Hanging it is the hard part

This is where most home setups go wrong. A heavy bag swinging and taking kicks puts far more force on the mount than its resting weight.

1. **Find a joist or beam.** Hang from structure, not drywall. In a garage, an exposed beam is ideal.
2. **Use a proper heavy bag mount** with a swivel, and a chain rated well above the bag's weight.
3. **Leave room to move.** You need space all the way around, plus clearance for the bag to swing.
4. **No suitable ceiling?** A [freestanding heavy bag stand](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=heavy+bag+stand) works. Long banana bags need a tall, heavy-duty one.
5. **Renting?** A stand, or skip the hanging bag and look at a free-standing bag.

## The gear that goes with it

- **Hand wraps.** Every session. They protect the small bones in your hand and wrist.
- **Bag gloves or training gloves.** 14 to 16 oz for most adults hitting a heavy bag.
- **Shins:** you can kick a banana bag barefoot, but build up slowly. Start with light, controlled kicks and add power over weeks, not days. Spinning kicks into a heavy bag at full speed are for people with years of practice.
