Mouth taping went from a fringe biohack to a social media staple in about three years, and a lot of people first heard about it on the Joe Rogan Experience.

The episode is **JRE #1506 with James Nestor**, which aired July 13, 2020. Nestor had just published *Breath: The New Science of a Lost Art*, and he and Rogan spent most of the conversation on one claim: most of us breathe badly, and breathing through your mouth at night makes it worse.

This page sticks to what was actually said, what the research has found since, and how to try it without doing something stupid.

> **The short version:** Nestor's advice on JRE was to use a **small, gentle piece of tape**, about the size of a postage stamp, if you sleep with your mouth open. A [2025 review of the research](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0323643) found little evidence it treats snoring or sleep apnea, and real risk if your nose is blocked. If you want to try it, use [gentle paper tape](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00ZQEL7O8) and read the [book](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0735213615) first.

## What Nestor actually said on JRE #1506

The core argument, as Nestor laid it out:

- **Your nose does work your mouth doesn't.** It slows, filters and humidifies air before it reaches your lungs, and it produces nitric oxide, which helps blood vessels open up.
- **Mouth breathing is common.** He put it at somewhere between a quarter and half of people breathing through their mouths habitually.
- **It compounds.** He linked chronic mouth breathing to snoring, sleep apnea and worse sleep in general, and argued that nasal passages you don't use tend to close up over time.
- **The tape is a training tool.** If you wake up with a dry mouth, a small piece of gentle tape across the lips nudges you back to nose breathing overnight.

He also gave a simple daytime drill: **breathe in for about six seconds, out for about six seconds**, through the nose.

What he did *not* say is that you should seal your mouth shut, or that tape fixes sleep apnea. That nuance mostly got lost on TikTok.

### Does Rogan do it himself?

We couldn't find a reliable source of Rogan saying he tapes his mouth every night. Several mouth-tape brands imply he does. That's marketing, not evidence. What's documented is that he hosted the conversation and took it seriously. We'd rather tell you that than invent a nightly routine.

## What the research found since

In May 2025, researchers published a **systematic review in PLOS ONE** that pooled 10 studies of mouth taping, covering 213 patients. The findings were underwhelming:

- Only **2 of the 6 studies** measuring sleep apnea severity found a meaningful improvement, and those were in people with **mild** sleep apnea.
- Most of the other studies found **no real difference**.
- Four studies raised the risk of **suffocation in people with nasal obstruction**, and researchers saw people "mouth puffing," breathing around the edges of the tape.

The authors' conclusion was blunt: there isn't enough evidence to recommend it, and doing it indiscriminately carries a real risk of harm.

That doesn't make Nestor wrong about nose breathing. It means tape is a weak tool for a medical problem. **If you snore loudly, stop breathing at night or wake up exhausted, get a sleep study.** Tape can hide the symptom of something that needs real treatment.

## If you still want to try it: the sensible way

1. **Check your nose first.** Close your mouth and breathe only through your nose for a few minutes while awake. If that's hard, stop here. Tape is not for you.
2. **Use gentle tape, and not much.** A small piece of [3M Micropore paper tape](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00ZQEL7O8), roughly stamp-sized, in the center of your lips. You should be able to open your mouth easily if you need to.
3. **Never use strong tape.** No duct tape, packing tape or athletic tape. The point is a reminder, not a seal.
4. **Practice awake first.** Wear it while reading for 15 minutes before you try a full night.
5. **Skip it when it's risky.** Colds, allergies, after drinking, after sedatives, and never on kids.

### Purpose-made mouth strips vs. paper tape

Brands sell pre-cut mouth strips, often with a vent in the middle so you're never fully sealed. They're more comfortable and less fiddly than cutting tape. They are not more "effective." The design that leaves a small opening is the safer choice, and it's closer to what Nestor actually described.
