Rogan's studio gym has a section most gym tours skip past: back and spine. Next to a Rogue reverse-hyper sit **two Teeter machines**, a classic inversion table and a stranger-looking thing called the DEX II.

According to Teeter, Rogan has said of the DEX II: "I use it every day... I swear by it." That's a strong endorsement. It also comes from the company that sells it, which is why this page separates what's in his gym from what the machines can actually do for you.

> **The short version:** Rogan has a [Teeter EP-560 Ltd. inversion table](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008054PFI) and a [Teeter DEX II](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000QSOSEA). The EP-560 is the standard pick for gentle traction at home. The DEX II is the one he reportedly uses daily, and it's more of a back-strength machine. Neither is safe for people with high blood pressure, glaucoma or heart problems.

## The EP-560: the standard inversion table

This is the inversion table most people picture. You strap in at the ankles, cross your arms and let the table tilt you back. A tether limits the angle, so you can stay at a gentle 20 to 30 degrees, which is where most people should stay, or go further once you're used to it.

It's FDA-registered as a medical device and UL safety-certified, which matters for a machine that holds you upside down by your feet. It's the most sensible starting point if your goal is light traction after a long day of sitting.

[Check the Teeter EP-560 Ltd. on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008054PFI)

## The DEX II: the one Rogan reportedly uses every day

The DEX II looks like a cross between a Roman chair and a reverse-hyper. You lie face-down with your hips supported and let your upper body hang forward, which gives a partial inversion and decompression. Then you can use the same machine for back extensions and core work.

That combination is the appeal. Hanging decompresses; extensions strengthen. Rogan's gym already leans heavily on back work, with the reverse-hyper right next to it, and the DEX II fits that pattern. It's a bigger, commercial-grade machine, and it costs more than the EP-560.

## Does inversion actually work?

The honest answer: **it can feel good, and the evidence that it fixes anything is limited.**

Inversion applies traction, gently pulling the spine apart. Some people with back pain, especially pain that eases when they hang from a bar, feel short-term relief. The research behind it is mostly small studies, and it doesn't change the underlying cause of most back pain. Strengthening work tends to have better long-term evidence, which is a point in the DEX II's favor.

If your back pain is new, severe, getting worse, or comes with numbness, weakness or trouble controlling your bladder, skip the gadget and see a doctor.

## Who should not invert

Hanging upside down raises the pressure in your head and eyes. Don't use an inversion table if you have:

- **High blood pressure** or heart disease
- **Glaucoma**, retinal problems or other eye-pressure conditions
- A history of **stroke**
- **Pregnancy**
- Recent **spinal surgery, fractures** or other acute injury

If you're unsure, ask your doctor before buying. Start shallow and short either way: a minute or two at a mild angle, then build up.

## Which one to buy

- **You sit all day and want gentle relief:** the [EP-560](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008054PFI). Simpler, cheaper, folds away.
- **You want to strengthen your back as well as decompress, and have the space:** the [DEX II](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000QSOSEA).
- **You're not sure you'll like inversion:** hang from a pull-up bar for 30 seconds a few times a week first. It's free, and it'll tell you if traction helps you.
