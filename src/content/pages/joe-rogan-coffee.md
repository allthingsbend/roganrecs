Joe Rogan has talked about enough coffee brands over the years that a lot of websites somehow conclude he has **four different current favorite coffees at the same time**.

That is not useful.

> **The short version:** **Black Rifle Coffee** is the clearest recent answer we can verify. If you want a straightforward cup, start there. If you want the more elaborate JRE-style functional coffee setup, Laird Superfood is the relevant add-in. Caveman Coffee is a real historical Rogan recommendation, and Four Sigmatic is the mushroom-coffee option.

**Last reviewed: September 6, 2026.** This page separates current evidence, historical Rogan mentions and our own buying recommendations.

## 1. Black Rifle Coffee: the strongest current answer

In **JRE #2170 with Max Lugavere**, Rogan identified the black coffee being served in the studio as **Black Rifle Coffee** and discussed using Black Rifle in a Laird-style setup.

That matters because it is direct evidence from Rogan talking about what was actually being served, not somebody scraping an old sponsor page.

Black Rifle founder Evan Hafer has also appeared on JRE repeatedly, which reinforces the long-running connection between the brand and the show.

## 2. Laird Superfood: the JRE-style coffee add-in

The Laird connection is more interesting than simply saying "Joe Rogan drinks Laird coffee."

Rogan described a setup that used **Black Rifle coffee with Laird-style ingredients including coconut oil, turmeric and cacao**.

So the useful distinction is:

- Black Rifle can be the coffee itself.
- Laird can be the add-in or functional-coffee component.

If that sounds good to you, [Laird Turmeric Superfood Creamer](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Laird+Superfood+Turmeric+Creamer) is an obvious product to compare.

### Should you buy it?

Buy it if you like the flavor and convenience.

Do not buy it because turmeric powder in coffee suddenly turns breakfast into a medical intervention.

A functional creamer can be a nice product. It is still creamer.

## 3. Caveman Coffee: real Rogan history

Caveman Coffee deserves to be here because Rogan really did discuss it positively in older JRE episodes.

In older conversations, he talked about the company's sourcing, freshness and connection with coffee farmers.

That is a stronger reason to include it than the generic "Joe Rogan once stood next to this product" logic that ruins recommendation sites.

You can [browse Caveman Coffee on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Caveman+Coffee).

### Is Caveman still his daily coffee?

We do not have enough evidence to say that.

Call it what it is: **a legitimate historical Rogan recommendation**.

That is still useful to fans. It is just not the same thing as current routine evidence.

## 4. Four Sigmatic mushroom coffee

Four Sigmatic has a long-running Rogan/JRE association and remains one of the brands people search when they ask about Joe Rogan mushroom coffee.

[Shop Four Sigmatic Mushroom Coffee on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Four+Sigmatic+Mushroom+Coffee)

### Is mushroom coffee better than normal coffee?

Not automatically.

If what you want is caffeine and coffee flavor, ordinary coffee is simpler and often cheaper.

Four Sigmatic makes more sense if you specifically like mushroom-coffee blends and want that product category. Do not assume a longer ingredient list means a better morning.

## Whole bean vs ground vs K-Cups

This matters more to the taste of your coffee than obsessing over which podcast guest mentioned which roast.

### Whole bean

Best if you own a grinder and care about freshness. Grind right before brewing.

### Ground

Best balance of convenience and normal coffee quality for most people.

### K-Cups

Fastest and easiest, but you give up some control over grind and extraction.

Black Rifle sells multiple formats, so buy the format your brewer actually uses instead of turning this into a gear project.

If convenience wins, [compare Black Rifle K-Cups](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Black+Rifle+Coffee+K+Cups).

## What brewing method makes sense?

You do not need Rogan's exact coffee machine to drink the same brand.

### Drip machine

Best for easy repeatable coffee, especially if more than one person drinks it.

### French press

Cheap, simple and full-bodied. Great for someone who wants whole-bean coffee without buying an espresso machine.

[Shop French presses on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=french+press+coffee+maker)

### Pour-over

Best if you actually enjoy the ritual and want more control.

### Espresso

Worth it if espresso itself is a hobby. Not worth buying a giant machine just to say your Black Rifle coffee touched pressure.

## Coffee before training

Rogan's broader routine often mixes caffeine, exercise, sauna and performance products, but you can simplify this to one useful question: **does caffeine help performance?**

For many people, yes. Caffeine can improve exercise performance and alertness.

The mistake is assuming that if some caffeine helps, heroic amounts must help more.

The FDA notes that **400 mg per day** is an amount not generally associated with negative effects for most healthy adults, though sensitivity varies a lot.

Coffee strength also varies wildly by bean, brew method and serving size, so "two cups" is not a precise dose.

If coffee improves your workout and does not wreck your sleep or make you feel terrible, great. If you are drinking a 4 p.m. cold brew and wondering why your sleep protocol stopped working, the mystery is not that deep.

## Is expensive coffee worth it?

Sometimes, but not because a famous podcaster drinks it.

Coffee is worth paying more for when you can actually taste the difference, care about sourcing, prefer a certain roast or value freshness.

It is not worth paying more because the bag has a skull, a mushroom, a mountain or a guy with a podcast on it.

Buy the coffee you like enough to brew consistently.
